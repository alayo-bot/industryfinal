Maps in Emergency Management

A historical timeline of geospatial tools in disaster response

1854
John Snow's Cholera Map
Hand-drawn dot map identifies Broad Street pump as cholera source — the first spatial analysis used to stop an active disaster.
1940s
Civil Defense Grid Maps
WWII standardizes large-format paper grid maps for evacuation routes, air-raid warden zones, and bomb-damage coordination.
1980s
GIS Enters the Field
FEMA adopts Geographic Information Systems, enabling digital layering of hazard zones, infrastructure, and population data.
2005
Katrina & Satellite Imagery
Hurricane Katrina drives adoption of real-time satellite imagery in command systems, revealing live flood extents and road closures.
2010s+
Web GIS & Mobile Maps
ArcGIS Online and mobile platforms deliver live dashboards to field responders, turning static maps into real-time command tools.
